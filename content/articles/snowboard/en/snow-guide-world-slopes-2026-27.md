## Intro

Snow changes every day, so no article can tell you exactly how deep it is right now. What this guide does is give you the map: when the big resorts across North America, Canada, Japan and Europe plan to open for the 2026/27 season, what each region is known for, how to read a snow report, and the places that **do** show live snow and avalanche conditions.

The opening dates come from the resorts and ski-industry sources listed at the bottom, checked on **31 October 2026**. Every one of them depends on the weather. Resorts open early when it snows and late when it does not, so always check the resort's own page before you travel.

## The season so far (end of October 2026)

- **Europe:** the glaciers are running. According to SnowBrains, **Sölden** and **Hintertux** in Austria opened on 2 October, along with the snow-farmed openings at **Levi** and **Ruka** in Finland, while **Saas-Fee**, **Titlis**, **Kitzsteinhorn** and **Kåbdalis** in Swedish Lapland followed during October.
- **North America:** **Arapahoe Basin** in Colorado was the first area to open for 2026/27, and **Keystone** opened just before Halloween.
- **Japan:** nothing is open yet. The big Hokkaido and Hakuba resorts plan to open from late November.

### The big weather story: El Niño

This winter is shaping up around a **strong El Niño**, a warming of the tropical Pacific that shifts weather patterns around the world. In September, OpenSnow wrote that there was a greater than 90% chance of a very strong event during autumn and winter 2026/27.

What that means for snow is less clear than people often think. OpenSnow points out that past strong El Niño winters gave very different results in the western USA: peak snowpack was 139% of normal in 1982/83 but only 73% in 1991/92. The most consistent signal is average to above-average snow in the US Southwest. Their advice: do not plan a whole season around a forecast. Book trips 7–10 days ahead, when you can see a stormy pattern coming.

## How to read a snow report

Every resort and snow site uses roughly the same terms:

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Base depth** (upper / lower) | How deep the settled snowpack is at the top and bottom of the mountain. A deep base means rocks and stumps are covered. |
| **New snow** (24 h / 48 h / 7 days) | Fresh snowfall. This is the number that means powder. |
| **Last snowfall** | When it last snowed. Ten days without new snow usually means hardpack and tracked-out runs. |
| **Lifts and runs open** | How much of the mountain you can actually ride. Early in the season this may be only a few runs. |
| **Surface** | Powder, packed powder, groomed, hardpack, icy, spring or wet snow. |
| **Avalanche danger** | A scale from **1 (low)** to **5 (very high)**, used in Europe, North America and Japan. It only matters off the groomed runs, but there it matters more than anything else. |

**Tip:** compare the resort's own report with an independent site. Resorts like to describe conditions in the best possible light.

## Canada

Western Canada is the heart of North American freeriding: big mountains, deep and often dry snow, cat-skiing and heli-skiing, and long seasons.

| Resort | Province | Known for | Planned opening 2026/27 |

| **Whistler Blackcomb** | British Columbia | North America's biggest resort, 8,171 acres, around 10 m of snow a year | 20–21 Nov (sources differ by one day) |

Whistler's season usually runs from late November into May, according to Tourism Whistler. The two Banff resorts often open very early because they sit high and cold.

## Japan

Japan has become one of the great snowboard destinations of the world for one reason: **japow**, Japanese powder. Cold air from Siberia picks up moisture over the Sea of Japan and drops it as light, frequent snow on Hokkaido and Honshu. Snowboarding is welcome almost everywhere, and tree riding is a big part of the culture.

| Resort | Area | Known for | Planned 2026/27 season |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Niseko United** (Grand Hirafu, Annupuri) | Hokkaido | The most famous powder in Japan | 28 Nov 2026 – 5 May 2027 |
| **Niseko Village and Hanazono** | Hokkaido | Part of the same mountain, Niseko Annupuri | From 28 Nov 2026 |
| **Rusutsu** | Hokkaido | Tree runs, quieter than Niseko | 28 Nov 2026 – end of March 2027 |
| **Furano** | Hokkaido | Dry snow in central Hokkaido | 28 Nov 2026 – 5 May 2027 |
| **Hakuba Goryu** | Hakuba, Nagano | First Hakuba resort to open | 21 Nov 2026 |
| **Hakuba Happo-one** | Hakuba, Nagano | Big Olympic mountain, steep alpine terrain | 1 Dec 2026 – 5 May 2027 |
| **Hakuba Tsugaike** | Hakuba, Nagano | Wide beginner-friendly slopes and good backcountry | 1 Dec 2026 |

The other Hakuba resorts open during December, depending on snow, according to Japan Ski Experience.

**Japan tip:** backcountry gates at Japanese resorts often have strict rules, and going beyond the boundary without permission can mean losing your lift pass. The **Japan Avalanche Network** publishes avalanche bulletins for areas such as Niseko, mostly in Japanese.

## Live snow status: where to check

These are the places to look for today's snow, not last month's.

**Independent snow reports and forecasts (worldwide)**

- [OnTheSnow](https://www.onthesnow.com): snow reports, base depths and opening dates for thousands of resorts.
- [Snow-Forecast](https://www.snow-forecast.com): snow forecasts and reports by resort, worldwide.
- [Skiresort.info](https://www.skiresort.info): snow reports and detailed resort tests, strong on Europe.
- [OpenSnow](https://opensnow.com): forecasts written by meteorologists, strong on North America, with Japan and Europe too.

**Official avalanche bulletins (always check before riding off-piste)**

- USA: [avalanche.org](https://avalanche.org), links to every regional avalanche center
- Canada: [Avalanche Canada](https://avalanche.ca)
- Europe: [EAWS](https://www.avalanches.org), which links to the national services in the Alps
- Switzerland: [SLF](https://www.slf.ch)
- Norway: [Varsom](https://www.varsom.no)
- Sweden: [Lavinprognoser](https://lavinprognoser.se)
- Japan: [Japan Avalanche Network](https://nadare.jp), mostly in Japanese

**The resort's own page** is the final word on which lifts and runs are open today. Most resorts also have webcams, and a webcam never exaggerates.

## Before you book

1. **Book late if you can.** Snow is impossible to predict months ahead. A week ahead is far more reliable.
2. **Go high early and late in the season.** Glaciers and high resorts (Sölden, Zermatt, Val Thorens, Banff Sunshine) are the safest bets in November and April.
3. **Japan peaks in January and February,** when the snowfall is heaviest.
4. **Europe's school holidays** (Christmas, February) mean crowds and high prices. Mid-January and March are often better value.
5. **Off-piste needs gear and knowledge:** an avalanche transceiver, shovel and probe, someone who knows how to use them, and a check of the day's avalanche bulletin.
