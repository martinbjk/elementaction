## Intro

Where should you go snowboarding? For years, the British snowboard magazine *Whitelines* answered that question with its [Resort Guide](https://whitelines.com/resort-guide): a list of resorts chosen for snowboarders and judged on four things, **park, powder, pistes and parties**.

This guide uses the same 26 resorts as a starting point. The Whitelines reviews were written several seasons ago, so we have checked every resort again against newer sources and added figures for the **2026/27 season**: altitude, kilometres of piste, number of lifts, snowparks and, where they have been published, opening and closing dates. The text is our own. For the original reviews, read Whitelines.

Two things to know about the numbers:

- **Many resorts share a lift system** with their neighbours. Méribel and Val Thorens are both part of Les 3 Vallées, for example. The figures below are for the **whole linked ski area**, which is what your lift pass usually covers.
- Altitude is the **highest lift-served point**, not the highest mountain nearby. Sources sometimes disagree by a few metres or lifts. Where they do, we say so.

## How to choose a resort

Start with the four Whitelines questions, then add two of our own:

1. **Park:** Do you want big jumps, a halfpipe, or a small park to learn your first boxes? Most big resorts have a park, but only a few have a full-size halfpipe.
2. **Powder:** Off-piste terrain matters more than piste kilometres if you ride a freeride board. Never ride off-piste without avalanche gear, training and an up-to-date avalanche forecast.
3. **Pistes:** A big linked area means more variety on hard-snow days.
4. **Parties:** Après-ski, bars and festivals. Some resorts are lively, others are quiet villages.
5. **Altitude and glaciers:** Higher resorts usually have more reliable snow early and late in the season. Glacier resorts like Saas-Fee, Zermatt and Les Deux Alpes can open in summer.
6. **Travel time:** A short transfer from the airport gives you more time on the snow.

If you are still choosing your board, read [How to choose the right snowboard](/en/snowboard/articles/snowboard-types-how-to-choose/). For opening dates by region and where to check live snow and avalanche conditions, see [Where the Snow Is: A Rider's Guide to the World's Slopes, 2026/27](/en/snowboard/articles/snow-guide-world-slopes-2026-27/).

## France

### Chamonix

Chamonix is not one ski area but several, spread along a valley under Mont Blanc. For snowboarders, the most famous is **Grands Montets** in Argentière: steep, high and known for freeride terrain, including the Rognons glacier. Grands Montets alone goes from **1,252 m to 2,765 m**, with **29 km** of pistes and **8 lifts**. A new **10-person gondola to Lognan** opens for 2026/27. Season: **28 November 2026 to 2 May 2027**. Chamonix is a serious mountain town. Much of the best riding is off-piste, so hire a guide if you don't know the terrain.

### La Plagne

One half of the **Paradiski** area, linked to Les Arcs since 2003 by the Vanoise Express cable car. La Plagne alone goes from **1,250 m to 3,250 m**, with about **225 km** of pistes. Note that the **Chiaupe glacier closed to skiing after the 2022/23 season**, but the Bellecôte glacier area remains. La Plagne is also home to the bobsleigh track from the 1992 Olympics, which will be used again in 2030.

### Les Arcs

The other half of Paradiski. The Les Arcs/Peisey-Vallandry area goes from **1,200 m to 3,226 m** at the Aiguille Rouge, with **200 km** of pistes and **52 lifts**. It has a freestyle park, and more than 2,000 m of vertical drop from the Aiguille Rouge to the lowest lift. Season: **12 December 2026 to 25 April 2027**.

### Les Deux Alpes

Les Deux Alpes has **the largest skiable glacier in Europe**, and its glacier opens for **summer riding, usually from June to September**, between about 2,800 and 3,600 m. That makes it a classic summer camp destination for freestyle riders. In winter, there are about **220 km** of pistes from around **1,300 m to 3,600 m**, a large snowpark with a **halfpipe, two boardercross courses and rails**, and the new **Jandri 3S** lift.

### Méribel and Val Thorens (Les 3 Vallées)

Méribel and Val Thorens are both part of **Les 3 Vallées**, the largest linked ski area in this guide: **1,100 m to 3,230 m**, **600 km** of pistes plus **50 km** of marked ski routes, and **159 lifts**. Season: **21 November 2026 to 2 May 2027**. The area has several parks: **Open Park** in Méribel, **VT Park** in Val Thorens, the **Pixel Area** and a boardercross in Les Menuires, and a **Family Park** in Courchevel. Val Thorens is the highest of the villages, which helps the snow at the start and end of the season.

### Morzine and Avoriaz (Portes du Soleil)

Morzine and Avoriaz are part of the **Portes du Soleil**, which crosses the border between France and Switzerland: **985 m to 2,254 m**, **580 km** of pistes and **163 lifts**. Season: **11 December 2026 to 18 April 2027**. Avoriaz has a long freestyle history, with snowparks and a halfpipe, and Les Gets has a boardercross. The weakness is altitude. Morzine is low, so snow in the village can be thin early and late in the season.

### Tignes and Val d'Isère

The two resorts share one lift system, sometimes called Espace Killy: **1,550 m to 3,456 m**, **300 km** of pistes and **80 lifts**, with runs partly on the **Grande Motte glacier**. The area's normal season runs from **late November to early May**, and part of the glacier also opens in **summer, normally from mid-June to mid-August**. Tignes has a snowpark at the top of the Grattalu chairlift, a free **airbag** for trying new tricks, and a **halfpipe** in Val Claret. Val d'Isère's park sits on Bellevarde. It has no halfpipe of its own, but a combined pass covers the one in Tignes.

## Switzerland

### Laax

The resort most snowboarders mention first. **Flims Laax Falera** goes from **1,100 m to 3,018 m**, with about **224 km** of pistes, **35 lifts** and **four snowparks**. The superpipe at **Crap Sogn Gion** is described by Snow-Online as the biggest halfpipe in Europe, and there is also a minipipe for learning. Laax also runs the **Freestyle Academy**, an indoor training centre of about **1,000 m²** with trampolines, a foam pit and an air track, so you can learn a trick before you take it to the snow.

### Saas-Fee

A car-free village surrounded by glaciers. The ski area goes from **1,800 m to 3,573 m**, with **101 km** of pistes and **23 lifts**. Saas-Fee is one of the few places in the Alps with a long **summer season on the Allalin glacier**, and the summer freestyle park there draws riders and national teams from around the world. In winter, the main park is **Snowpark Morenia**. The 2026/27 season runs from **18 July 2026 to 18 April 2027**.

### Verbier (4 Vallées)

Verbier is the best-known resort in the **4 Vallées**: **1,350 m to 3,330 m**, **412 km** of pistes and **65 lifts**. Season: **7 November 2026 to 25 April 2027**. It is famous for steep freeride terrain and for the **Xtreme Verbier** contest on the Bec des Rosses face. For park riders, there is the **Swatch Snowpark** in Verbier and the **Centralpark** in Thyon. It is also a busy, upmarket resort, so book early.

### Zermatt

Zermatt shares its ski area with **Breuil-Cervinia and Valtournenche** in Italy, under the Matterhorn: **1,562 m to 3,899 m**, the highest in Europe in this guide, with **322 km** of pistes, **38 km** of ski routes and **51 lifts**. The area is listed as open **all year**, thanks to the summer ski area on the **Theodul glacier**. It has three parks: **Snowpark Zermatt**, the **Indianpark** in Cervinia, and a **Funslope**. Like Saas-Fee, Zermatt is car-free.

## Austria

### Damüls

A small village in the Bregenzerwald region of Vorarlberg, and an unusual choice in a guide like this. It is here because of **the snow**. Damüls-Mellau goes from **710 m to 2,050 m**, with **76 km** of pistes, about **18 km** of ski routes and **21 lifts**. Skiresort.com gives it a **top rating for snow reliability** and for its snowpark. The general season runs from **early December to mid-April**. There are few big hotels and little nightlife, so come for the riding, not the parties.

### Mayrhofen

Mayrhofen in the Zillertal is known for two things: the **PenkenPark**, with five areas for riders of all levels, and **Snowbombing**, a spring music festival held in Mayrhofen since 2005. The ski area goes from **630 m to 2,500 m**, with **142 km** of pistes and **23 lifts**, according to the Tyrol tourist board. It also has **Harakiri**, often called Austria's steepest piste, with a gradient of **78 percent**.

### St Anton (Ski Arlberg)

St Anton is part of **Ski Arlberg**, which also includes St. Christoph, Stuben, Lech, Zürs, Warth and Schröcken: **1,304 m to 2,811 m**, about **300 km** of pistes and **85 lifts**. Season: **2 December 2026 to 18 April 2027**. It is famous for off-piste terrain and for its après-ski. The **Stanton Park** snowpark is on the Rendl, and there are also parks in Lech and Warth.

## Rest of Europe

### Courmayeur, Italy

On the Italian side of Mont Blanc, opposite Chamonix. The ski area is fairly small, about **42 km** of pistes from **1,224 m to 2,755 m**, but the upper slopes have excellent off-piste terrain. The **Skyway Monte Bianco** cable car takes you up to Punta Helbronner at about **3,466 m**, the start of high-mountain freeride routes. That terrain is glacier terrain, so go with a mountain guide.

### Grandvalira, Andorra

One of the largest ski areas in the Pyrenees, and usually cheaper than the Alps: **1,710 m to 2,640 m**, **215 km** of pistes and **75 lifts**. Season: **5 December 2026 to 4 April 2027**. There are two snowparks, **Snowpark El Tarter** and **Snowpark Xavi** in Grau Roig. Andorra also has low sales taxes, which helps keep the price of equipment down.

### Trysil, Norway

**Norway's largest ski resort** by length of pistes, about **70 km**, served by **31 lifts**, with a base at about **450 m**. Trysil is a family-friendly, easy-going resort in the forests of eastern Norway, close to the Swedish border. It is the obvious choice for a weekend trip from Sweden. Expect cold, dry snow and short days in midwinter.

### Jasná, Slovakia

Jasná, on both the north and south sides of **Chopok** in the Low Tatras, is often called the largest ski area in Central Europe. It goes from **900 m to 2,024 m**, with about **34 km** of pistes including freeride zones. It has a snowpark, **steep couloirs on the north side** and wide open slopes on the south side. It is much cheaper than the Alps.

## Long haul

### Breckenridge, USA

In Colorado. The summit is at **3,962 m** and the base at about **2,900 m**, so you will feel the altitude. The resort has **2,908 acres** (about 1,180 hectares) of terrain, **35 lifts** and about **25 acres** of terrain parks. Breckenridge hosted the first winter **Dew Tour** in 2008 and held it every December until the contest moved to Copper Mountain in 2020. Owner: Vail Resorts.

### Mammoth Mountain, USA

In California's Sierra Nevada. Mammoth is famous for its parks: **eight Unbound terrain parks**. It has **3,500 acres** (1,420 hectares), **25 lifts** and an average snowfall of about **10 metres** a year. The season usually runs from **November to May**, and in big snow years it can last into summer. The summit is about **3,369 m**. Owner: Alterra Mountain Company.

### Whistler Blackcomb, Canada

One of the biggest resorts in North America: two mountains, **8,171 acres** (3,307 hectares), **34 lifts** and an average snowfall of about **11.7 metres** a year. The **Peak 2 Peak gondola** links the mountains with an unsupported span of **3.02 km**. The top lifts reach about **2,240 m** on Blackcomb, and the vertical drop is over **1,500 m** on each mountain. Whistler hosted the alpine skiing events of the **2010 Winter Olympics**. Owner: Vail Resorts.

### Niseko, Japan

On the island of Hokkaido, Niseko is famous around the world for light, dry powder. **Niseko United** is four resorts on Mount Niseko Annupuri, which is **1,308 m** high: **Grand Hirafu, Hanazono, Niseko Village and Annupuri**. Together they have **30 lifts** and **73 trails**, linked near the summit. Niseko has a **gate system** for off-piste riding: you may only leave the resort through official gates, and the rules are strict. For more on Japan's season, see our [snow guide](/en/snowboard/articles/snow-guide-world-slopes-2026-27/).

### Cardrona, New Zealand

Near Wānaka, on New Zealand's South Island, about 50 minutes' drive from Queenstown. The season runs in the **southern winter**, so you can ride while Europe has summer. Cardrona built **New Zealand's first international halfpipe in 1989** and has **seven freestyle areas**: two halfpipes, four parks and a boardercross course. The resort covers about **615 hectares**, with **9 lifts**. Owner: RealNZ.

### Sun Peaks, Canada

In British Columbia, about **56 km** northeast of Kamloops. Sun Peaks is the **second-largest ski resort in Canada** by terrain, about **4,270 acres** (1,730 hectares), but much quieter than Whistler. The base is at about **1,198 m**. Sources disagree on the summit and the number of lifts: Wikipedia gives a summit of **2,152 m** in one place and 2,080 m in another, so check the resort's own trail map before you go.

## Before you go

- **Check the dates.** Season dates are the resorts' plans and can change with the snow. Check the resort's own website a week before you travel.
- **Check the avalanche forecast** every day you ride off-piste, and carry a transceiver, shovel and probe. Our [snow guide](/en/snowboard/articles/snow-guide-world-slopes-2026-27/) lists the avalanche services by region.
- **Look at the park reports.** Parks are rebuilt every season, and the features change through the winter.
- **Read the original reviews.** For the reasoning behind the list, see the [Whitelines Resort Guide](https://whitelines.com/resort-guide).
