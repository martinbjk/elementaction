## Intro

Surfing and skateboarding spread through films as much as through magazines. A surf film shown in a school hall in the 1960s, or a skate video copied from VHS tape to VHS tape in the 1990s, could change what a whole generation thought was possible.

This list covers three kinds of films: **surf films**, **skate videos** made by the companies themselves, and **documentaries and feature films** about the culture. Years, directors and awards are checked against the sources at the bottom.

## Surf films

### The Endless Summer (1966), Bruce Brown

The film that defines the surf film. Bruce Brown followed two young Californians, **Mike Hynson** and **Robert August**, around the world in search of summer and the perfect wave: Senegal, Ghana, Nigeria, South Africa, Australia, New Zealand, Tahiti and Hawaii. It was made for about **$50,000** and earned more than **$20 million** worldwide. The break they found at **Cape St. Francis** in South Africa became world-famous through the film. In **2002**, the Library of Congress added it to the US National Film Registry. A sequel, *The Endless Summer II*, followed in 1994.

### Morning of the Earth (1972), Albert Falzon and David Elfick

The Australian answer to the Californian surf film: dreamy, without narration, about surfers living simply on the coast. It was filmed in Australia, Hawaii and Bali, and it recorded the first surfers to ride **Uluwatu**, which helped put Bali on the surf map. Its soundtrack was the **first Australian soundtrack album to go gold**.

### Five Summer Stories (1972), Greg MacGillivray and Jim Freeman

The last surf film from the duo who later became famous for IMAX films. It mixes the joy of surfing with the political and environmental worries of the early 1970s, and features Gerry Lopez, David Nuuhiwa and Eddie Aikau. The poster was a painting by surf artist **Rick Griffin** (see our [surfboard art history](/en/surfing/articles/surfboard-art-history/)).

### Big Wednesday (1978), John Milius

A Hollywood drama about three surfing friends in California from 1962 to 1974, ending with a legendary swell. Milius, a surfer himself, co-wrote it with Denny Aaberg. It flopped on release: according to The Inertia, it earned about $4.5 million, roughly half its budget. On home video in the 1980s it became a cult classic, and many surfers now call it the most honest surf feature film. Gerry Lopez plays himself in the final sequence.

### Point Break (1991), Kathryn Bigelow

An FBI agent (Keanu Reeves) goes undercover as a beginner surfer to catch a gang of bank robbers led by a surfer-philosopher (Patrick Swayze). Not a realistic surf film, but a hugely popular one, and its story grew out of Kem Nunn's novel *Tapping the Source* (see our [books list](/en/inspiration/articles/surf-skate-books/)).

### Step into Liquid (2003), Dana Brown

Made by Bruce Brown's son, Dana. It shows how many different kinds of people surf, from kids in Texas riding tanker wakes to big-wave riders at **Cortes Bank**, an underwater reef far off the California coast.

### Riding Giants (2004), Stacy Peralta

The history of big-wave surfing, from **Greg Noll** in Hawaii in the 1950s and 1960s to **Laird Hamilton** and tow-in surfing. It was the **first documentary ever to open the Sundance Film Festival**. Read the modern chapter of the story in [Nazaré and Mavericks](/en/surfing/articles/big-wave-surfing-nazare-mavericks/).

## Skate videos

### The Search for Animal Chin (1987), Powell-Peralta

The Bones Brigade (Tony Hawk, Steve Caballero, Mike McGill, Lance Mountain and Tommy Guerrero) travel through Hawaii, California, Nevada and Mexico looking for "Won Ton" Animal Chin, the mythical first skateboarder. It was **one of the first skate films with a story**, not just tricks to music. The famous **Chin Ramp**, a double half-pipe with a spine and a tunnel, was torn down after filming and rebuilt for the film's 30th anniversary.

### Video Days (1991), Blind Skateboards

A 24-minute video directed by a young **Spike Jonze**, with Mark Gonzales, Guy Mariano, Rudy Johnson, Jordan Richter and **Jason Lee**. It is often called the most important skate video of all time, and it still holds up when you strip it back to the skating. Jason Lee's part caught the eye of director Kevin Smith, who cast him in *Mallrats*, the start of his acting career.

### Yeah Right! (2003), Girl Skateboards

Directed by **Ty Evans** and **Spike Jonze** for Girl and Chocolate. It is remembered for its "invisible skateboard" scenes, filmed with a green screen, and a cameo by actor Owen Wilson, in which Eric Koston in a wig does the trick Wilson seems to land.

### Fully Flared (2007), Lakai

Also by Ty Evans and Spike Jonze, for the shoe company Lakai. Its opening, in which skaters ride through a series of explosions, is one of the best-known intros in skate video history. Huck Magazine calls it "the now legendary exploding opening sequence".

## Documentaries and feature films

### Dogtown and Z-Boys (2001), Stacy Peralta

The story of the Zephyr team from Santa Monica and Venice in the 1970s, who brought surf style to empty swimming pools. Directed by Peralta, himself a Z-Boy, and narrated by **Sean Penn**. It won both the **Audience Award and the Directing Award at Sundance** in 2001. A feature-film version, **Lords of Dogtown** (2005), was directed by Catherine Hardwicke from a script by Peralta, with Heath Ledger and Emile Hirsch.

### Bones Brigade: An Autobiography (2012), Stacy Peralta

The story of the team Peralta put together in the early 1980s, when skateboarding was in decline: Tony Hawk, Rodney Mullen, Steve Caballero, Lance Mountain, Tommy Guerrero and Mike McGill. It premiered at Sundance in 2012. For more on the people, see our [Skateboarding Hall of Fame article](/en/skateboard/articles/skateboarding-hall-of-fame-museum/).

### Mid90s (2018), Jonah Hill

Actor Jonah Hill's debut as a director, released by A24. A 13-year-old (Sunny Suljic) in **1996 Los Angeles** finds a new family in an older crew of skaters at a skate shop. It premiered at the Toronto International Film Festival.

### Minding the Gap (2018), Bing Liu

Bing Liu started filming his friends skating in **Rockford, Illinois**, as a teenager. Over many years, the footage became a documentary about growing up, violent homes, race, class and fatherhood. It won a special jury award at Sundance and was **nominated for the Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature**. It is a film about skateboarding, but even more about the people who skate.
