## Intro

Skateboarding in 2026 is two things at once. It is an **Olympic sport** with world rankings, national teams and medal targets. It is also a **street culture** built on small brands, skate shops, video parts and magazines. The two worlds overlap, but they are moving in different directions.

This article looks at the whole picture: how big the industry is, which companies matter in the USA and in Europe, who governs the sport, how good skaters make money, why Asia wins so much, and finally who actually profits when skateboarding grows. Sources are linked directly in the text. Where the facts are uncertain or the sources disagree, we say so. In a few places I add information from people I know in the skate world, and I say so where it appears. There is a short note about me at the end of the article.

*Updated 18 November 2026 with a section on the European skate industry, a clearer guide to how reliable the numbers are, and a new analysis of who profits from the growth.*

## In short

- **The market** for skateboards is *estimated* at about **US$3.6 billion** a year. The estimate comes from commercial research firms and should be treated as rough.
- **Vans**, the biggest skate brand, has seen its revenue fall from **$4.2 billion to $2.1 billion** in four years. This is a *company figure*.
- **Participation** in the US has grown: about **9.9 million** Americans skated in 2025, according to *survey data*. But fewer skaters enter contests, and many shops are struggling.
- **Europe** has built its own scene of skater-owned brands such as **Polar** and **Palace**, but it still depends on American hardware and a few large distributors.
- **Japan** dominates Olympic and world contests. At the first LA28 qualifier in Rome in June 2026, Japanese skaters took **7 of 12** podium places.
- **World Skate**, a roller sports federation based in Switzerland, governs Olympic skateboarding. The culture itself is still shaped by brands, media and skaters.
- **A pro career is possible**, but only a small group earns a good living from skateboarding alone.
- **Who profits?** Mostly big retailers with their own brands, sportswear and fashion groups, contest owners and investors, and the athletes and coaches inside the Olympic system. Independent shops, small brands and most pros get a much smaller share.

## How reliable are the numbers?

Not all numbers in this article are equally solid. To make the difference clear, we mark the most important figures in the text as a *company figure*, *official result*, *survey data* or *estimate*.

| Type | Examples in this article | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| **Company figure** (annual reports, earnings calls, filed accounts) | Vans' revenue, Zumiez' sales, Palace's turnover, the price of Supreme | High. The company has reported it, often audited. |
| **Official result** | Olympic medals, world championships, Asian Games | High. |
| **Survey data** | 9.9 million US skaters, 8.5 million in Brazil | Medium. Depends on how the survey asked and who answered. |
| **Trade press and interviews** | Deck prices, pro pay, shop closures | Medium. Reported by journalists, not audited. |
| **Estimate** (commercial market research) | The $3.6 billion world market and its growth rate | Low. Sold as paid reports, and the method is not public. |

## How big is the skateboard industry?

Nobody publishes an official world figure for skateboarding. The numbers that exist come from market research firms that sell reports. Two of them, [Fortune Business Insights](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/skateboard-markt-109870) and [Expert Market Research](https://www.expertmarketresearch.com/reports/skateboard-market), both put the global skateboard market at **about $3.59 billion in 2025** (*estimate*), growing at roughly 3.5 to 3.7% a year. According to Fortune Business Insights, **North America** is the biggest region, with about 38% of the market, and **Asia-Pacific** is growing fastest.

These figures mostly count boards and hardware. Skate shoes and clothing, which are much bigger businesses, are counted elsewhere. Two firms arriving at almost the same number does not make it more reliable: neither publishes its method. Treat it as an order of magnitude, not a measurement.

The more reliable numbers are about people. In the US, about **9.9 million** people skateboarded in 2025, up from 9.3 million the year before (*survey data*), according to [Statista's compilation of US participation data](https://statista.com/statistics/191308/participants-in-skateboarding-in-the-us-since-2006).

## The biggest companies

Skateboarding has a few large companies and hundreds of small ones.

**Shoes and apparel: the big money**
- **Vans** (owned by VF Corporation since 2004, when VF paid $396 million) is the best-known skate brand in the world. After a long boom, its revenue fell from **$4.2 billion in fiscal 2022 to $2.1 billion in fiscal 2026** (*company figure*), according to [SGB Executive](https://sgbonline.com/exec-the-post-covid-rocky-history-of-vans-and-a-vf-ceo-look-at-whats-ahead/). VF's turnaround plan has brought new management and layoffs. In May 2026 VF [reported](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/20/vf-vfc-q4-2026-earnings-call-transcript/) that Vans had returned to growth in its Americas direct-to-consumer sales for the first time in almost four years, but it still expects a decline for the brand in fiscal 2027.
- **Nike SB** and **Adidas Skateboarding** are skate divisions of the world's two largest sportswear companies. Their skate-specific revenues are not published.
- **Supreme**, founded as a New York skate shop, was bought by VF in 2020 for about $2.1 billion and [sold to the eyewear group EssilorLuxottica](https://www.retaildive.com/news/vf-sells-supreme-essilorluxottica/721639/) in 2024 for $1.5 billion (*company figures*).

![Bar chart: Vans annual revenue fell from $4.2 billion in fiscal 2022 to $2.1 billion in fiscal 2026](./chart-vans-revenue.png)

**Hardware: decks, wheels and trucks**

The hardware business is smaller and run largely by long-established American distributors. Fortune Business Insights names **NHS** (Santa Cruz, Independent, Creature), **Skate One** (Powell-Peralta, Bones), **Globe International** and **Bravo Sports** among the key players. Other important groups include **Deluxe** (Spitfire, Thunder, Real, Anti-Hero) and **Crailtap** (Girl, Chocolate).

Most decks are not pressed by the brand that sells them. They come from a handful of **woodshops**. According to a [woodshop guide by the UK shop NOTE](https://noteshop.co.uk/blogs/post-office/woodshop-guide-skateboard-decks), **BBS** presses decks in Mexico for brands such as Baker, Anti-Hero and Polar. **Control** is in Quebec, Canada, **HLC** is in the Basque Country in Spain, and **Dailan Huahong** is in China. Production has largely moved to China and Mexico.

**Retail**

**Zumiez**, the biggest skate-oriented chain, sold for **$929.1 million** in the year to January 2026 (*company figure*), [up 4.5%](https://shop-eat-surf-outdoor.com/news/zumiez-posts-strong-q4-and-full-year-results-eyes-25-store-closures-in-2026/617647/) on the year before. Most skate hardware, though, is still sold through independent skate shops.

## The European skate industry

For a long time Europe was mainly a market for American brands. That has changed. Europe now has its own skater-owned brands that are respected worldwide, but the business behind them is still small, fragmented and dependent on a few distributors.

**How European brands broke through.** In a [2018 Jenkem article](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/2018/12/03/how-european-skate-brands-are-taking-over/), two distributors, Wes Morgan of UK-based Rock Solid Distribution and Oli Merkelbach of German-based Beast Distribution, described the timeline. In the late 2000s the two most influential European brands were **Blueprint** from the UK and **Cliché** from France. **Flip**, which started in the UK, moved to California instead. According to Merkelbach, American brands relied on large distributors to get their products into European shops but did little else to connect with European skaters. That left room for brands that represented their own cities and riders.

**Polar Skate Co. (Sweden).** Polar was founded in **2011** in Malmö by the skater and filmmaker **Pontus Alv**. He told Jenkem that he started it after feeling sidelined on a big American shoe brand's European team: "We always felt as if we were just some Eurotrash." Polar sells decks and clothing worldwide and is still skater-owned. Its sales figures are not public: the Swedish company [Polar Skate Co Sweden AB](https://www.ratsit.se/5595167429-Polar_Skate_Co_Sweden_AB) was registered in Malmö in 2025 and had not yet filed any annual accounts. Alv has also co-founded the shoe brand **Last Resort AB** with designer Sami Tolppi. Jenkem [described it](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/?p=87470) as an independent alternative to the big shoe brands, a field where newcomers usually need large investors.

**Palace Skateboards (UK).** Palace was [founded in London in 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_Skateboards) by Lev Tanju and friends, and has stores in London, New York, Los Angeles, Tokyo, Seoul and Hong Kong. Since 2014 it has had an [ongoing partnership with adidas](https://www.grailed.com/drycleanonly/palace-skateboards-history). Palace is one of the few European skate brands whose numbers are public: in its latest accounts filed at Companies House, **Palace Skateboards Ltd** reported a turnover of about **£34 million** and 82 employees (*company figure*, [via Endole](https://open.endole.co.uk/insight/company/08253929-palace-skateboards-limited)). Palace shows that a skate brand from Europe can become a global streetwear name, built as much on clothing and collaborations as on boards.

**Other European names.** **Magenta** from Paris reached American shops together with Palace and Polar through the importer Theories of Atlantis, according to Jenkem. **Carhartt WIP**, a German-based company [founded in 1994](https://us.carhartt-wip.com/en-gb/history) that adapts the American workwear brand for Europe, runs its own international skate team and more than 80 stores worldwide. The UK's **Isle** and the Amsterdam label **Pop Trading Company**, which [grew out of distributing small European skate brands](https://bonkers-shop.com/blogs/news-1/introducing-pop-trading-company), are other examples of the European independent scene.

**Distributors: the hidden middle.** Between brands and shops sit the distributors, and they are consolidating.
- **Centrano**, which [started as a single shop in Denmark](https://www.boardsportsource.com/centrano-distribution-talks-covid-skateboarding-boom-the-olympics/), grew into one of Europe's biggest distribution companies.
- **Shiner** in the UK [took over the distribution of Element hardgoods](https://www.boardsportsource.com/?p=148170) in the UK, Ireland, the Nordics, France, Spain, Italy, the Benelux, Austria and Germany from October 2025.
- **OneTurn** was formed in 2025 by the former heads of Liberated Brands Europe, to take over Liberated's European business after the bankruptcy. In May 2026 it [added etnies, éS and Emerica](https://shop-eat-surf-outdoor.com/?p=619243) to its portfolio.
- **HLC** in Spain both presses and distributes decks.

**What Europe lacks.** Back in 2018, Wes Morgan pointed out that Europe had "no truck companies, and there's not really any big wheel companies". Europe's strength is board brands, clothing and culture. Trucks, wheels and most decks still come from American companies and factories in Mexico and China.

**Retail in Europe.** The German online retailer **Skatedeluxe**, [founded in 2004](https://skatevideosite.com/companies/skatedeluxe), sells across Europe and has its own in-house line. The Austrian chain **Blue Tomato** belongs to the American Zumiez and has just under 90 stores in nine countries. Zumiez' international sales, which come from Europe and Australia, [fell 4.2%](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/zumiez-q4-earnings-call-highlights-2026-03-12/?.tsrc=rss) in fiscal 2025 when currency effects are removed (*company figure*), while North America grew.

## The threats

**1. The post-pandemic hangover.** Skateboarding boomed during the 2020 lockdowns. When demand fell back, the market was left with too much stock and heavy discounting, according to shop owners interviewed by [Bubble Skateboard Magazine](https://bubbleskatemag.com/a-few-theories-why-skateboarding-got-old-and-is-not-cool-anymore).

**2. Private equity and broken companies.** [The American Prospect](https://prospect.org/2025/08/21/2025-08-21-private-equity-ripped-heart-out-of-skateboarding/) describes how investment firms bought and resold skate companies. Dwindle Distribution (Almost, Enjoi, Blind) was sold by Globe to a subsidiary of Transom Capital for $1.5 million in 2019 and had effectively collapsed by 2023. **Liberated Brands**, which ran Billabong, Quiksilver, Volcom and other surf and skate brands under licence from Authentic Brands, filed for bankruptcy in February 2025 with more than $220 million in debt. It closed all its stores, and more than 1,000 jobs were lost ([SGI News](https://sginews.com/Content/after-liberated-bankruptcy-abg-names-new-boardriders-licensees.aspx)).

**3. Tariffs.** In 2025 the US introduced [new tariffs](https://shredder.news/tariffs-raise-skateboard-prices-aug-1-2025/) on imports from China, Mexico and Canada, the three countries where most skate hardware is made. Shops and brands expected higher prices. According to [Jenkem](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/2025/09/04/in-this-economy-are-more-skaters-buying-shop-decks/), average deck prices had already risen to around **$75–85**, and shops reported more "sticker shock" among customers.

**4. Closing skate shops.** Independent shops are under pressure from rent and costs. Bubble reports a shop in Bordeaux whose rent has quadrupled in 20 years. In Germany, **Titus**, the skate chain founded in 1978 by skateboarding pioneer Titus Dittmann, [filed for insolvency](https://fashionunited.de/nachrichten/business/skateboard-handler-titus-ist-insolvent/2025021060205/) in February 2025. Another company, 24/7 Distribution, [took it over](https://fashionunited.de/nachrichten/business/24-7-distribution-ubernimmt-titus-eine-legende-lebt-weiter/2025080162802) in July 2025 and closed six of its shops. In the UK, Bristol's **Fifty Fifty** [closed](https://www.surfertoday.com/skateboarding/skate-shop-closure-sign-of-the-times) in November 2025 after 28 years.

**5. Too many brands, too few buyers.** Several shops and writers point to a market with more brands than it can support, where each one sells too few boards.

**6. Fewer kids competing.** The contest platform [The Boardr](https://theboardr.com/post/skateboarding_growth) found that of 29,667 skaters who entered contests since 2017, **64% had not competed in the last three years**, and participation fell after 2023.

## Skateboarding is changing

The sport itself is changing in several ways.

- **It is getting younger.** At Tokyo 2020, Momiji Nishiya [won street gold](https://olympics.com/en/news/13-year-old-nishiya-momiji-wins-first-ever-women-s-street-skateboarding-gold) at **13**, with 13-year-old Rayssa Leal second. In Paris, China's [Zheng Haohao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Haohao) competed at **11**. At the 2026 [Park World Championship](/en/skateboard/articles/park-world-championship-asuncion-2026/), both winners, Mizuho Hasegawa and Egoitz Bijueska, were **15**.
- **Park and contest skating have grown.** Many young skaters, especially in Japan, learn in skateparks and through coaching and lessons. Skate schools are growing. One German school organiser told Bubble his programmes had grown about 20% in a year.
- **More girls skate.** Women's events are now equal parts of the Olympic programme. In the UK, the number of young women choosing skateboarding for the Duke of Edinburgh's Award [rose from 39 in 2017 to 353 in 2022](https://www.dofe.org/?p=110440).
- **The core scene feels older.** Bubble argues that shop owners, brand managers and team riders are ageing, that teenagers have many other options, and that the core street scene and the Olympic scene now live in parallel.

So is skateboarding growing or shrinking? The honest answer is both. The number of people who skate in the US is rising, while contests, shops and the big brands are under pressure. Who benefits from that is a different question, which we return to at the end.

## Professionals and amateurs: can you make a career?

Yes, there are professionals and amateurs, but in skateboarding the line is drawn by sponsors, not by a league.

**The ladder**
1. **Flow:** free products from a brand, but no money.
2. **Amateur (am):** on a team, with products and sometimes a small monthly payment. [Surfer Today](https://www.surfertoday.com/skateboarding/how-pro-skateboarders-earn-money-and-build-their-careers) puts it at about $500–1,000 a month. Many ams have other jobs.
3. **Professional:** a skater gets a **pro model**, a board or shoe with their name on it, and earns royalties from it.

**How pros make money**
- **Board royalties:** a share of every board sold with their name on it. According to [Jenkem](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/2022/05/03/16-things-didnt-know-professional-skateboarding/), some pros earn up to about **$10 per board** sold directly through the brand's website, where the margin is higher.
- **Shoe deals:** the biggest income for most top pros. According to Jenkem, a signature shoe with a large company can still pay a six-figure sum, but million-dollar deals from the start are no longer typical. A colourway, choosing the colours for an existing shoe, usually pays about $5,000.
- **Energy drinks and lifestyle sponsors** such as Monster and Red Bull.
- **Contests:** [Street League Skateboarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_League_Skateboarding) (SLS), founded by Rob Dyrdek in 2010, has paid the biggest prize purses in skateboarding. In 2011 the total was $1.6 million, and the winner took $200,000. Some sponsors pay **bonuses that match contest winnings**.
- **Olympic funding:** national federations now fund their best athletes. [Skateboard GB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboard_GB), for example, received about **£1.67 million** from UK Sport for the Paris cycle.
- **Social media and video:** YouTube, sponsored posts and the skater's own merchandise.

**The reality.** Most pros are independent contractors without health insurance, according to Jenkem. Careers are short, often 5–10 years, according to Surfer Today. Only a small group earns millions, such as Nyjah Huston, who by 2013 had already won more contest prize money than any other skater. For everyone else, skateboarding is often a mix of sponsorship, coaching, filming, working in the industry and other jobs.

**Two routes to the top.** Today there are two ways to "make it". The **video route** builds a name through video parts, magazines and brand teams. The **contest route** runs through national teams, world rankings and the Olympics. A few skaters, like Yuto Horigome, do both.

## Who governs skateboarding?

Formally, Olympic skateboarding is run by **World Skate**. Its official address is in Lausanne, Switzerland, but the federation is run from Italy. An acquaintance of mine who visited the Lausanne address and knocked on the door found that it only receives post, which is forwarded to the staff in Italy. [World Skate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Skate) began in 1924 as the international federation for **roller skating**, and in 2017 it merged with the International Skateboarding Federation. It governs roller hockey, speed skating, scootering and many other wheeled sports as well. It has 130 member federations. Its president, the Italian **Sabatino Aracu**, has led the federation since 2005. He was [confirmed for a new term](https://www.worldskate.org/news/3655-in-rome,-the-ws-congress-confirms-sabatino-aracu-as-president-of-the-world-skate-federation.html) at the congress in Rome in September 2024, and was re-elected at the World Skate Congress in Asunción, Paraguay, in October 2026, held during the World Skate Games.

World Skate organises the **World Skateboarding Tour**, the world championships and the Olympic qualification system. Under it are national federations, such as Skateboard GB in the UK and Sveriges Skateboardförbund in Sweden.

**Who really decides?** It depends what you mean.
- **Olympic rules and qualification:** the IOC and World Skate.
- **Big contests:** often private companies. SLS is owned by investors led by Lorenzo Fertitta's Fiume Capital, and ESPN [sold majority control of the **X Games**](https://fortune.com/2022/10/26/espn-sells-x-games-winter-private-equity-firm-msp-sports-capital) to the investment firm MSP Sports Capital in 2022.
- **What counts as good skateboarding, who is cool, which tricks matter:** still the culture itself, meaning skaters, brands, filmers and media such as Thrasher.

The road to the Olympics was not smooth. When skateboarding was added for Tokyo in 2016, rival skateboarding federations [fought over the right to run it](https://www.sportsdestinations.com/sports/action-sports/skateboarding-world-disarray-despite-new-olympic-11601), with lawsuits, before the IOC gave the job to the roller sports federation. Many skaters were also sceptical. In Sweden, the head of the national federation [told SVT](https://www.svt.se/sport/skateboard/skejtare-skeptiska-till-os-deltagande) in 2016 that many were worried skateboarding's uniqueness would disappear if it became too much like other sports.

**LA 2028.** Skateboarding is on the programme again, with park and street for men and women, and **22 athletes per event**. Qualification started in June 2026 and runs until June 2028, with a maximum of three skaters per country in each event ([Surfer Today](https://www.surfertoday.com/skateboarding/la-2028-olympic-skateboarding-qualification-system)).

### North America

**USA.** The US is still the heart of skateboarding. Most of the big brands, distributors, magazines and video productions are American, and about **9.9 million** Americans skated in 2025. The big contests are there too: Street League, the X Games and Tampa Am. But in contests, the US has fallen behind: in Tokyo and Paris it won **five of 24 Olympic medals and no gold**, and **no American woman** has yet won an Olympic skateboarding medal. In a [World Skate column](https://www.skateboarding.worldskate.org/news/1861-currents-team-usa-s-date-with-destiny-at-la28.html), Niall Neeson writes that the USA relies on its "embarrassment of professional skateboarding riches", while Brazil and Japan have more organised national selection. At **LA 2028**, in California where the sport took off, the US will be skating at home.

**Canada.** Canada's biggest role is in the wood. Most decks are made from **hard rock maple**, which [grows slowly](https://rippedlaces.com/skateboarding/what-are-skateboard-deck-made-of/) in the cold winters around the Great Lakes, in Canada and the northern US. Woodshops such as **Control** in Quebec press decks for brands including Poetic Collective and Studio. In contests, Canada [sent four skaters to Paris](https://develop.olympic.ca/2024/06/26/four-team-canada-skateboarders-will-take-to-the-park-and-streets-of-paris-2024/), including 14-year-old **Fay De Fazio Ebert**, who won women's park gold at the 2023 Pan American Games. She has said Canada lacks high-level training facilities.

**Mexico.** Mexico is a manufacturing country for skateboarding. The large woodshop **BBS** presses decks there for many well-known American brands, which is why the 2025 US tariffs on Mexico worried the industry. Mexico City has a [big street scene](https://www.latinpost.com/articles/153630/20220117/skateboarding-scene-in-mexico-city-grows-as-parks-for-the-sport-emerge.htm). The government began building skateparks in the 1980s, and brands have followed: Nike SB built an Aztec-inspired park in the city in 2014, and Vans opened a House of Vans there in 2020. In contests, Mexico is still small. According to The Boardr, **88%** of Mexican contest skaters had stopped competing, one of the highest rates.

### South America

**Brazil.** Brazil is the second great skate nation of the Americas. A 2023 [survey by Ibope Repucom](https://maquinadoesporte.com.br/skate/ibope-repucom-brasil-ganhou-20-milhoes-de-fas-de-skate-desde-2019) estimated that **8.5 million** Brazilians skate and more than 47 million follow the sport (*survey data*). Brazil has won five Olympic medals: Rayssa Leal (silver in Tokyo at 13, bronze in Paris), Kelvin Hoefler, Pedro Barros and Augusto Akio. The **SLS Super Crown** final is held in São Paulo, and at the first Pan American Games skateboarding contests in Santiago in 2023, Brazil won both street golds, Rayssa Leal and Lucas Rabelo ([World Skate](https://www.skateboarding.worldskate.org/news/1635-pan-american-games-2023-street-and-park-results.html)).

**The rest of South America.** Peru's **[Angelo Caro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngelo_Caro)** finished fifth in Tokyo, took silver at the 2023 Pan American Games, and was second at the 2025 world street championship in São Paulo, held in March 2026. Colombia's Jhancarlos González won bronze at the Pan Am Games. In October 2026, **Paraguay** hosted the World Skate Games in Asunción. South America is producing more contest skaters every year, often from public skateparks rather than private training centres.

### Asia

**Japan** is the strongest contest nation in the world (see the next section). At the **2026 Asian Games** in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan won **7 of 12** skateboarding medals ([World Skate](https://www.skateboarding.worldskate.org/news/1871-asian-games-2026-park-and-street-results.html)).

**China** makes a large share of the world's skate hardware and has built a national team from scratch since 2017. At the 2026 Asian Games, **Zhu Yuanling** won women's street gold and Cui Chenxi took bronze.

**South Korea** formed its national team only in **January 2022**, with six skaters all under 16, and the [Korea Times reported then](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/southkorea/globalcommunity/20220823/koreas-skateboarding-scene-needs-more) that no park in the country was equipped for professional training. Four years later, **Kang Jun-i**, 17, [won Korea's first Asian Games skateboarding gold](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/sports/20260927/asian-games-s-korea-wins-gold-in-mens-street-skateboarding-at-asian-games) in men's street and took silver at the World Championship in Asunción.

**Chinese Taipei** won two park silvers at the 2026 Asian Games.

**India** is at an [early stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboarding_in_India). It has new skateparks such as Janwaar Castle, a rural park where children skate for free on the condition that they go to school, and a 44,000-square-foot park in Chandigarh opened in 2023. No Indian skater has yet qualified for the Olympics.

### Oceania

**Australia** is the world's best park nation at the Olympics. **Keegan Palmer** won park gold in both Tokyo and Paris, and **Arisa Trew** won women's park gold in Paris at 14, becoming Australia's youngest Olympic gold medallist ([Ben Waterworth](https://www.benwaterworth.com/post/sport-wrap-australia-skates-its-way-to-most-successful-olympic-campaign)). Street skater **Chloe Covell** won the first LA28 qualifier in Rome in June 2026.

### Europe

Europe has a strong culture, many world-class skaters and, as described above, its own brands. But it does not have Japan's depth in contests. Highlights:
- **France:** [Aurélien Giraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aur%C3%A9lien_Giraud) was street world champion in 2023, and Paris hosted the Olympic skateboarding at Place de la Concorde in 2024.
- **Great Britain:** Sky Brown has won Olympic bronze in both Tokyo and Paris.
- **Spain:** Egoitz Bijueska became park world champion in 2026 at 15. The Basque Country is also home to the woodshop HLC.
- **Portugal:** [Gustavo Ribeiro](https://olympics.com/en/news/street-skateboard-gustavo-ribeiro-self-belief-dreams) won the SLS Super Crown in 2022, the first Portuguese skater to do so.
- **Sweden:** Malmö is a model skateboarding city and the home of Polar (see the next sections).

### Africa

**South Africa** is Africa's leading contest nation in skateboarding and sent skaters to both Tokyo and Paris. [Boipelo Awuah](https://africa.espn.com/espn/story/_/id/40636753/south-africa-skater-boipelo-awuah-olympic-debut-looms-pelvic-fracture-tokyo), from Kimberley, qualified for women's street in Paris after a pelvic fracture kept her out of Tokyo. Local skaters [report](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/south-africa/2026-08-11-south-african-female-skateboarders-are-carving-out-space-in-a-growing-sport/) more girls at skateparks, but also limited resources and poorly organised women's contests. The charity **[Skateistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateistan)**, founded in Kabul in 2007, runs skate schools in South Africa, Cambodia and Afghanistan for more than 6,000 children a week, 54% of them girls. A film about its Kabul school won an Oscar.

## Why does Asia dominate?

Above all, it is **Japan** that wins, with **China**, **South Korea** and **Chinese Taipei** catching up. In the [street world final in Asunción](/en/skateboard/articles/street-world-championship-asuncion-2026/), South Korea's Kang Juni took silver. In Rome in June 2026, China's Cui Chenxi took bronze in women's street. These are the explanations given in the sources:

1. **Perfect timing.** Japan's national coach, Daisuke Hayakawa, has said that the growth of Japanese skateboarding and the Olympics "crossed paths at just the right time". He [told Olympics.com](https://olympics.com/en/news/what-are-secrets-behind-japan-dominance-in-skateboarding) that from around 2010, social media and YouTube let Japanese kids see world-class skating and aim to be number one in the world, not just in Japan.
2. **A contest pathway.** Japanese skaters were sent to international contests early. In 2018, the big American amateur contest **Tampa Am** held a qualifier in Japan, which Sora Shirai won. He went on to become world number one.
3. **Skateparks and coaching.** Street skating is less common in Japan, so most kids learn in skateparks, with instructors, from a young age. World Skate's Niall Neeson told Olympics.com that this means they learn the basics earlier.
4. **Parents and status.** Skateboarding used to be seen as a pastime for troublemakers in Japan. After Tokyo, it became a sport kids take lessons in, with parents driving them to the park and buying new boards. Hayakawa [told the South China Morning Post](https://scmp.com/sport/outdoor/article/3268908/street-culture-paris-olympics-how-japans-skateboarding-youth-turned-tide) that the number of skaters had **tripled** since Tokyo.
5. **They keep going.** According to The Boardr's data, only **27%** of Japanese contest skaters had stopped competing, against 69% in the US and 84% in Australia.
6. **State programmes in China.** After the Olympic decision, China opened six regional training camps in 2017 and recruited athletes from gymnastics, martial arts and other sports through its sports school system, according to [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1008029). Skateboarding is now part of [China's National Games](https://info.newsgd.com/node_28ab3f3552/ad6d372fea.shtml).

The numbers show the result. At the first LA28 qualifying event in Rome in June 2026, Japanese skaters took **7 of the 12** podium places ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michellebruton/2026/06/26/la28-skateboard-park-street-standings-after-first-olympic-qualifier/)). According to a [World Skate column](https://www.skateboarding.worldskate.org/news/1733-currents-the-japanese-armada.html) in 2025, Japanese skaters held the top four places in the men's street world ranking and seven of the top ten in women's street.

## What has happened to skateboarding in Europe?

Europe has a strong skate culture, but its industry and its elite results have both been under pressure.

**The good news: skateparks, grassroots and brands**
- **Malmö** in Sweden has become a [model for how a city can support skateboarding](https://huckmag.com/article/vans-malmo-skate). It has a municipal skateboard coordinator, the Bryggeriet skatepark (opened in 1998 in an old brewery), and **Bryggeriets Gymnasium**, a high school built around skateboarding.
- In Sweden, skateboarding [became a member of the national sports confederation (RF)](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/se/riksidrottsforbundet/pressreleases/skateboard-ny-idrott-i-riksidrottsfoerbundet-870078) in **2013**. At the time, the Swedish federation, Sveriges Skateboardförbund, had about 12,000 members and estimated there were about **100,000 skaters** in Sweden.
- Olympic status has brought money. Skateboard GB got about £1.67 million from UK Sport for Paris 2024, up from £166,825 in 2018.
- European skater-owned brands such as Polar and Palace are respected worldwide.
- Skate schools and girls' skateboarding are growing in several countries.

**The bad news: shops, events and costs**
- Germany's biggest skate retailer, **Titus**, went insolvent in 2025 and closed six shops. The UK's **Fifty Fifty** closed after 28 years.
- Shops in France and elsewhere report rents and costs rising faster than sales. A Spitfire wheel costs about €70 in Europe, compared with about $55 in the US, according to Bubble.
- **CPH Open**, the famous Copenhagen festival when skaters took over the city's streets, [ended in its original form in 2018](https://boardworld.com.au/blogs/news/cph-open-the-final-chapter) because the crowds had become a safety risk. The festival [has been held again since](https://doseskateboarding.com/articles/cph-open-2023), for example in 2023.

**Elite results.** European skaters win medals: Sky Brown won bronze for Great Britain in both Tokyo and Paris, and Spain's 15-year-old **Egoitz Bijueska** became park world champion in 2026. But Europe does not have Japan's depth. No European country won an Olympic skateboarding gold in Tokyo or Paris.

## Who actually profits from the growth?

*This section is our own analysis, built on the facts in the article. Others may read the same facts differently.*

The question is not only whether skateboarding grows or shrinks. More people skate, but the money does not spread evenly. Looking at who gains and who loses, a pattern appears.

**Who gains**

1. **Retailers that own their own brands.** At Zumiez, the biggest skate chain, its own private-label products made up **30% of sales in 2025**, [up from 12% five years earlier](https://shop-eat-surf-outdoor.com/?p=617647) (*company figure*). When a chain sells its own brand, it keeps the margin that would otherwise go to an independent skate brand. Shop decks are the same idea on a smaller scale.
2. **Sportswear and fashion groups.** The biggest money in skateboarding is in shoes and clothing, and the biggest players there are Nike, Adidas and VF. Palace's partnership with adidas and Supreme's billion-dollar sale show that a skate brand's value is often realised in fashion. When Supreme was sold, the money went to its owners and investors, not to the skate scene it came from.
3. **Owners of contests and media.** SLS and the X Games are owned by investment firms. More viewers and Olympic attention make those assets more valuable.
4. **Athletes, coaches and federations inside the Olympic system.** Public money has arrived: Skateboard GB's funding rose roughly tenfold between 2018 and the Paris cycle. Skate schools and coaching are growing. This money mainly benefits the small group of contest skaters and the organisations around them.
5. **Large distributors.** As brands struggle, distribution is moving to fewer and larger companies, such as OneTurn and Shiner in Europe. Whoever controls the route into the shops gains bargaining power.

**Who gets a smaller share**

- **Independent skate shops**, squeezed by rent, discounting, online sales and chains with their own brands.
- **Small and mid-sized core brands**, which face too many competitors, rising costs and tariffs on the hardware they import.
- **Most pro skaters**, who work as contractors without insurance, while the biggest shoe deals have become smaller.
- **New and young skaters**, who pay more: a deck costs around $75–85 in the US, and a set of wheels about €70 in Europe.

**What it means.** Growth in the number of skaters does not automatically mean growth for the core skate industry. The money tends to end up with those who own the customer relationship (big retailers and fashion brands), with those who own the assets (investors in contests and brands), and with the Olympic system, which is now partly funded by the state. The skater-owned brands in Europe, such as Polar and Last Resort AB, are an attempt to keep more of the value inside skateboarding. Whether that model can grow without outside investors is one of the big open questions for the next few years.

## The future

Based on the trends above, these are the most likely directions. They are our assessment, not facts:

1. **More Olympic structure.** LA 2028 will put skateboarding in front of a huge American audience, and federations will keep building national teams and coaching programmes.
2. **Asia will keep winning** for years. Japan's skaters are young and do not quit, and China is investing through its state system.
3. **The industry will consolidate.** Weak brands will close or merge, distributors will grow larger, prices will stay high, and shops will lean more on lessons, events and their own shop decks.
4. **The big brands must win back skaters.** Vans' recovery is the key question. VF itself expects the decline to continue into 2027.
5. **Two cultures, one board.** The Olympic sport and the street culture will keep existing side by side. For most skaters, skateboarding will stay what it has always been: something you do with your friends, not for medals.
