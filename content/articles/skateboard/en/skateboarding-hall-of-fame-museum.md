## Intro

In Simi Valley, north-west of Los Angeles, there is a building full of skateboards: clay-wheel boards from the 1960s, homemade boards, fibreglass and urethane from the 1970s, and pro models from every decade since. It is the **Skateboarding Hall of Fame and Museum**, founded by **Todd Huber**, and it is also where skateboarding honours its legends.

This article tells the story of the museum and the Hall of Fame, explains how inductees are chosen, and lists **every inductee from 2009 to 2026**, with a short note on each.

## The museum: from a garage to the world's first skate museum

According to Yahoo Lifestyle, Todd Huber started collecting skateboards in his garage at the age of 24, after quitting smoking, when a friend suggested he buy a skateboard. Over about thirty years the hobby grew into what the article calls the world's first skateboarding museum.

Some facts about the museum:

- **Founded:** 1997, according to Wikipedia and Surfer Today. For many years it was housed at the **Skatelab** skatepark in Simi Valley.
- **New home:** In **December 2018** the museum reopened in a 10,000-square-foot space in the **Simi Valley Town Center**.
- **The collection:** Huber estimated in 2019 that it holds about **5,000 pieces**, though he has never counted them one by one. Many of the boards are used, customised or homemade, from times when skateboards could not always be bought.
- **What you see:** According to Surfer Today, the museum describes its collection of folk-art skateboards as the largest in the world. There are also historical artefacts, memorabilia, a library, VHS tapes and an indoor skate ramp.
- **Non-profit:** The Hall of Fame is a 501(c)(3) non-profit organisation.

## The Hall of Fame: how inductees are chosen

The first class was inducted in **2009**: **Tony Hawk, Tony Alva, Danny Way and Bruce Logan**. Since then a new class has been inducted almost every year, with no class in 2011.

According to Surfer Today, a **68-member nomination committee** chooses the nominees. The final vote involves around **400 people**: skateboarding professionals, the nomination committee, earlier inductees, and members of the media and industry.

Inductees are grouped by era and category:

- **Era categories:** 1960s, 1970s (two groups), 1980s (two groups), 1990s and 2000s, depending on when the skater made their mark.
- **Icon:** people, companies, magazines, bands and places that shaped skate culture without being competitive skaters, such as Thrasher, Black Flag, Powell Peralta or the Pipeline skatepark.
- **Women:** a category for the women who built skateboarding.
- **Photographer:** the photographers who documented the sport.

There is also a **Lifetime Achievement Award**, given in 2026 to **Rich Novak** (co-founder of NHS, the company behind Santa Cruz and Independent), **George Powell** (Powell Peralta and Bones Wheels) and **Steve Van Doren** (Vans).

## Why it matters

Skateboarding is young, and much of its history lives in the memories of the people who were there, in old magazines and in boards hanging in garages. The Hall of Fame and Museum is one of the few places where it is collected, preserved and shown to the public, and where the riders of the 1960s and 1970s get the same recognition as today's stars.

For more on some of the inductees, read our articles on [LA punk and skateboarding](/en/skateboard/articles/la-punk-skate-scene-1980s/) (Z-Boys, Black Flag, Suicidal Tendencies, Thrasher), [the history of skateboard graphics](/en/skateboard/articles/skateboard-graphics-history/) (Wes Humpston, Jim Muir, Powell Peralta, Jim Phillips, Steve Rocco) and [the photographers who framed board culture](/en/inspiration/articles/board-culture-photographers/) (Warren Bolster, Glen E. Friedman, J. Grant Brittain).
