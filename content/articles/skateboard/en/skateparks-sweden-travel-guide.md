## Intro

Sweden is not the first country most skaters think of for a skate trip. It should be on the list. Over the last twenty years, Swedish towns, from big cities to villages with a few thousand people, have built public concrete skateparks. Most of them are public parks run by the local municipality.

This guide is for skaters who want to find them. Below is a **searchable list** of Swedish concrete parks, with address, year built, size, builder, terrain and whether the park has lights. After the list you'll find practical tips for skating in Sweden: when to come, what to do when it rains, how to get around and how to pay.

The park data comes from [Skateparksguiden](https://www.skateparksguiden.se/), a knowledge bank about Swedish concrete parks linked to the Swedish Skateboard Federation. We have rewritten the descriptions, sorted the parks by region and added map links and filters.

## Why Sweden is worth the trip

**A concrete country.** Sweden's first concrete skateparks were built in the early 2000s. Fun Skatepark in Linköping (2003) is described by Skateparksguiden as [one of the first concrete parks in Sweden](https://www.skateparksguiden.se/fun-skatepark), and Alingsås Skatepark (2004) was [one of the very first concrete skateparks built in Sweden](https://www.vastsverige.com/en/alingsas/produkter/alingsas-skatepark/). Most of the parks in this guide followed in the 2010s: of the parks with a known year, 32 were built between 2010 and 2019.

**Big, free and spread out.** The parks with a known size cover more than 33,000 m² in total. The median park is around 800 m², and the biggest are Collini Plaza in Bollnäs (2,300 m²), Halmstad Arena Skatepark (2,000 m²) and Leif Park in Leksand (1,700 m²). They are spread across the whole country, from Skåne in the south to Luleå near the Arctic Circle.

**A mix of terrain.** More than half of the parks in the list (24 of 40) have a bowl, and most combine a bowl with a street section. There are also flow parks with no right angles, like Lomma, and plaza-style street parks, like Söderlyckan in Lund and Steel Park in Luleå.

**A small group of builders.** Many Swedish parks were built by the same few specialist companies. In our list, Pivotech appears on six parks, and Concreatures, AKEA, ENR and Nord Skateparks on several more. If you like one park, you may well like its siblings: search the list by builder name.

**Cities that plan for skating.** Malmö has become a reference for skate-friendly urban planning, with its giant Stapelbäddsparken and the LOVE Malmö plaza. We wrote about it in [Skateboarding and the City](/en/inspiration/articles/skateboarding-and-the-city/).

## Planning your trip

### When to come

Outdoor concrete parks are a **spring-to-autumn** thing. From roughly late autumn to early spring, rain, frost and snow make most outdoor parks unskateable, especially in central and northern Sweden. The best months are usually **May to September**, when days are long and the parks are dry.

The light matters as much as the weather. In summer, evenings stay bright late into the night, especially in the north. In autumn and winter it gets dark early, which is when the **"Lit at night"** filter in the list becomes useful: 27 of the 40 parks with full details have lights.

### When it rains: indoor halls

Sweden has an unusually dense network of indoor skate halls, many run by local clubs. Lundby Park in Borås, for example, sits right outside the town's indoor hall, so you can move inside if the weather turns. For checked addresses of the big halls, such as Bryggeriet in Malmö, Fryshuset in Stockholm and Hangaren in Linköping, see our list of [verified indoor skateparks](/en/skateboard/articles/verified-indoor-skateparks-world/). It also has a section on smaller Swedish halls.

Many indoor halls are run by associations and may need membership or a day pass. Check opening hours before you go.

### Getting around

Most towns in this guide can be reached by train or regional bus. The national rail operator [SJ](https://www.sj.se/en) runs long-distance trains, and each region has its own public transport company with its own app and tickets, for example [SL](https://sl.se/en) in Stockholm, [Västtrafik](https://www.vasttrafik.se/en/) in the Göteborg region and [Skånetrafiken](https://www.skanetrafiken.se/en/) in Skåne.

A tip for route planning: use the region filter. Skåne and West Sweden have the highest density of parks, so a week there can cover many parks with short train rides in between.

### Paying for things

Sweden is one of the most cashless countries in the world. According to the Riksbank, Sweden's central bank, [Sweden and Norway have the lowest amount of cash in circulation as a share of GDP in the world](https://www.riksbank.se/en-gb/payments--cash/payments-in-sweden/payments-report-2025/trends-on-the-payments-market/the-swedish-payments-market-is-almost-entirely-digital/), and in 2025 only [5 percent of people paid cash for their most recent purchase in a shop](https://www.riksbank.se/globalassets/media/rapporter/betalningsrapport/2026/engelsk/press-conference-slides-payment-report-2026.pdf). Bring a card or a phone with a payment app; cash is accepted in fewer and fewer places. The mobile payment app Swish is very common, but it requires a Swedish bank account.

### Park etiquette

- **Shared parks.** Many Swedish municipal parks are open to skateboards, BMX bikes and scooters alike; Alingsås, for example, [welcomes all three](https://www.vastsverige.com/en/alingsas/produkter/alingsas-skatepark/). Expect to share the space, and look out for younger kids.
- **Clean up.** Most parks are free and run by the municipality. Keeping them clean helps keep them open.
- **In an emergency, call 112.**

## About the data

- **Source:** Every park in the list comes from [Skateparksguiden](https://www.skateparksguiden.se/), read in November 2026. Each card links back to the original page.
- **Two levels of detail:** 40 parks have full details. Another 39 towns are listed by Skateparksguiden, including big cities like **Stockholm, Göteborg (seven parks), Malmö, Uppsala, Norrköping and Örebro**. We have not yet added their details, but each town in the list links to its Skateparksguiden page. They will be added in an update.
- **Some fields are missing** where Skateparksguiden does not give them, for example the size of Bohus and Falköping or whether Bromölla is lit.
- **Not a complete list.** Sweden has many more skateparks than this guide covers, including wooden and modular parks and indoor halls. Skateparksguiden focuses on concrete parks.
- **No secret spots.** This guide only lists public skateparks. We do not publish street spots.
- **Errors and tips:** Parks change, get renovated or close. If you find an error or know a park that is missing, write to tips@elementaction.com.
