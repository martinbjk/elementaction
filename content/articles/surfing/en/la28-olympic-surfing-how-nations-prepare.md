## Intro

At the Los Angeles Olympics in July 2028, surfing will be held at **Lower Trestles** in San Clemente, one of the most consistent high-performance waves in California. Only **48 surfers**, 24 men and 24 women, will get to compete. With so few places, the race to get there has become a contest between national systems as much as between surfers.

This article looks at how the main surfing nations are preparing: how much money they have, how they are organised, how many surfers are in their programmes, and what they do to bring young talent through qualification. Sources are linked directly in the text. Where we could not find a public figure, we say so.

## In short

- **48 places, 2 per country and gender.** A third place is only possible through the team ranking at the World Surfing Games.
- **The qualification rules changed twice in 2026** after protests from the pro surfers. The latest version gives **8 places per gender** to the World Surf League's Championship Tour.
- **Four places are already decided** (provisionally). Japan's Kanoa Igarashi and Mirai Ikeda won the Asian Games, and Australia (women) and Peru (men) won the team places at the 2026 World Surfing Games.
- **Australia has the most money.** Surfing Australia had revenue of about **US$8.4 million** in 2025, more than twice that of any other surfing federation for which figures are available.
- **Brazil** gets about **R$11.4 million** from the national lottery via its Olympic committee in 2026.
- **The USA** only regained its official surfing federation in 2026, after more than four years without Olympic funding.
- **Great Britain** gets **£1.375 million** for the whole four-year cycle, a fraction of what the big surfing nations spend.

## How the numbers should be read

We mark the most important figures as an *official figure* (published by a government, Olympic committee or federation), *reported* (from journalists or the athletes, not audited), or an *estimate*. Budgets are rarely comparable between countries: some cover only the elite team, some cover the whole federation, and some are one year while others cover four.

## How to qualify for LA28, and the fight over it

The International Surfing Association (ISA) runs Olympic surfing, while the best surfers in the world compete on the World Surf League's Championship Tour (CT). How many places each of them controls has been the big political question of 2026.

**The first version (February 2026).** The ISA proposed that only **five surfers per gender** would qualify through the CT, while the 2028 World Surfing Games would give **10 places per gender**, and each country could send three surfers per gender ([Carve](https://www.carvemag.com/2026/02/surfers-protest-olympic-rule-changes/)). In Paris 2024, 10 men and 8 women had qualified through the CT. World champion **Yago Dora** and other top surfers [criticised the new system](https://www.thestar.com.my/sport/others/2026/02/23/surfing-world-champion-says-many-top-tour-surfers-unhappy-with-olympic-qualification-tweaks), saying their concerns had been ignored. The Portuguese federation's president called it a "disastrous" process ([Record](https://www.record.pt/modalidades/surf/detalhe/fps-opoe-se-ao-recuo-no-processo-de-qualificacao-olimpico-para-los-angeles-2028)). Brazil's federation, Surf Brasil, on the other hand, [supported the change](https://jornaldebrasilia.com.br/torcida/surf-brasil-apoia-nova-proposta-de-classificacao-para-olimpiadas-de-2028/).

**The revised version (in force since mid-2026).** After the criticism the system was changed again ([The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/surf/isa-revises-olympic-qualification-system-criticism/)). The version dated 29 June 2026 ([official qualification document](https://olympic.org.na/uploads/documents/SRF-LA28-Qualification-System-170726-054216.pdf)) looks like this, per gender:

| Route | Places per gender | When |
|---|---|---|
| WSL Championship Tour ranking (max 1 per country) | 8 | August 2027 – June 2028 |
| 2028 ISA World Surfing Games (max 1 per country) | 7 | 2028 |
| Asian Games | 1 | September 2026 |
| Pan American Games, Lima | 1 | 2027 |
| European Surfing Championship | 1 | 2027 |
| Africa and Oceania (best placed at the 2027 World Surfing Games) | 1 each | 2027 |
| Best team at the 2026 World Surfing Games | 1 | November 2026 |
| Best team at the 2027 World Surfing Games | 1 | May 2027 |
| Host country (USA), if not already qualified | 1 | – |
| Universality place for a smaller surfing nation | 1 | – |
| **Total** | **24** | |

Each country can qualify a maximum of **two surfers per gender**. The only way to get a third is to win the team ranking at the 2026 or 2027 World Surfing Games (*official figure*). The places are ranked in a hierarchy: higher-ranked routes are allocated first, so a provisional place from a continental event can still disappear if the same country fills its places through the CT and the 2028 World Surfing Games.

## Places already decided

- **Asian Games, September 2026:** Surfing made its Asian Games debut in Tahara, Japan. **Kanoa Igarashi** beat Indonesia's I Made Pajar Ariyana in the men's final, and **Mirai Ikeda** beat China's 17-year-old Yang Siqi in the women's final ([New Kerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/asian-games-17-year-old-yang-loses-ikeda-shortboard-as-365.htm)). Both became the first surfers to [provisionally qualify for LA28](https://www.theinertia.com/?p=331010).
- **World Surfing Games, Peru, November 2026:** 14-year-old **Leihani Zoric** won the women's gold and Ethan Ewing the men's, giving Australia the team title and the extra women's place. Peru won the men's team place after Lucca Mesinas and Miguel Tudela both made the final ([Reuters via Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/international/4007231-surfing-australians-zoric-ewing-take-world-surfing-games-gold)). Zoric became the [youngest-ever ISA world champion](https://thesiliconreview.com/2026/11/australia/surfing-gold-zoric-ewing-world-games-australian-surfers).
- **Next:** the 2027 World Surfing Games in El Salvador, 22–30 May 2027 ([The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/surf/australia-peru-punch-tickets-la-2028-olympics/)).

## Country by country

### Australia: the best-funded surfing nation

- **Money:** According to [The Inertia](https://www.theinertia.com/surf/australia-found-recipe-usher-new-era-surfing-dominance/), Surfing Australia had revenue of about **US$8.4 million (A$12 million)** in 2025, "by far" the most of any national surfing federation. The next-best funded is probably Brazil's, with about US$3.2 million (*reported*). A large part comes from the government: the Australian Sports Commission's grants list shows an agreement worth **A$7,975,000** (including GST) for **December 2024 to June 2026** (*official figure*, [ASC grants list 2024](https://www.sportaus.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0006/1181175/Calendar-Year-2024-Grants-List.pdf)). For the six months before that, the Commission allocated A$1,000,000 for high performance, A$75,000 for talent pathways and A$225,000 for participation ([ASC allocation 2024–25](https://sportaus.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/1145864/Investment-allocation-2024-25-Website-as-at-07-May-2024.pdf)). Surfing is also part of the government's [A$513 million high-performance package](https://surfingaustralia.com/australias-513-million-investment-into-high-performance-sport/) for the next two years.
- **Organisation:** A national high-performance programme with coaches, a nutritionist, strength and conditioning, a sports psychologist and a high-performance centre on the Gold Coast. The money also pays for training camps, surf trips and wave-pool sessions (The Inertia).
- **Team size:** **50 nationally categorised athletes** in the high-performance programme in 2025, a number that changes each year ([Surfing Australia](https://surfingaustralia.com/?p=229609)). The national team is called the **Irukandjis**.
- **Talent:** The new **Talent UNLSHD** programme supports young girls through state-run delivery across regional Australia ([Surfing Australia](https://surfingaustralia.com/?p=250414)). The juniors won team gold at the [2025 ISA World Junior Championship](https://surfingaustralia.com/back-to-back-world-championsthe-irukandjis-win-gold-at-the-2025-isa-world-junior-championships/), and Leihani Zoric, who came through the programme, is now world champion at 14.

### Brazil: lottery money and the "Brazilian Storm"

- **Money:** Brazil's Olympic committee (COB) passes on lottery money to the national federations. In 2026 surfing gets **R$11.399 million**, almost the same as skateboarding (*official figure*, [Máquina do Esporte](https://maquinadoesporte.com.br/los-angeles-2028/cob-aprova-orcamento-de-r-6786-milhoes-para-2026-e-repassa-42-as-confederacoes/)). The federation's total revenue was about US$3.2 million in 2025, according to The Inertia (*reported*).
- **Organisation:** The federation, now called Surf Brasil, selects the team together with the COB. If Brazil wins an extra team place, the COB and the federation decide who gets it ([Band](https://www.band.com.br/nacional/brasil/brasil-busca-vagas-extras-para-surfistas-na-olimpiada-de-2028)). Brazil is already [planning a training camp at Lower Trestles](https://dukesurf.com/en/Brazil-is-already-preparing-the-Olympic-training-camp-in-Lowers-and-all-the-logistics-for-2028/).
- **Team size:** **Six surfers** at the 2026 World Surfing Games: Tainá Hinckel, Luana Silva, Laura Raupp, Miguel Pupo, Ítalo Ferreira and Yago Dora (Band). Brazil sent six surfers to Paris, more than any other country ([Itatiaia](https://www.itatiaia.com.br/esportes/olimpiada/surf-federacao-muda-regra-de-classificacao-a-olimpiada-e-brasil-pode-ser-afetado/)).

### USA: the host that lost its federation

- **Organisation:** USA Surfing gave up its status as national governing body in **December 2021**, after an audit by the US Olympic & Paralympic Committee (USOPC) made "many negative findings" about its finances ([NBC](https://www.nbcsportsbayarea.com/olympics/2028-los-angeles/usa-surfing-national-governing-body-status/1931552/?amp=1)). For more than four years it ran without USOPC funding. U.S. Ski & Snowboard, led by former WSL chief Sophie Goldschmidt, tried to take over the sport, but withdrew. The USOPC [recertified USA Surfing](https://www.nbcwashington.com/olympics/2028-los-angeles/usa-surfing-national-governing-body-status/4091446/) from **1 June 2026**, calling it financially stable. Its latest public tax return shows how small the federation is: revenue of **US$865,896** in 2024, most of it from contest entry fees and other programme income, and only US$107,657 in donations (*official figure*, [Form 990 via ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/814742350)). The private backing announced in 2025 is not yet included.
- **Money:** USA Surfing secured multi-year private backing from the housing developer Kamaka, which also plans a training wave pool, and the surf company Resin Services ([AP via Fox Sports](https://www.foxsports.com/articles/soccer/usa-surfing-gets-new-financial-backing-in-a-bid-to-be-recertified-for-its-hometown-olympics)). Amounts have not been published.
- **What an American Olympian earns:** USOPC medal bonuses of $37,500 for gold, $22,500 for silver and $15,000 for bronze, plus a promised **$200,000** per US Olympian from the financier Ross Stevens' $100 million gift, half paid later in life and half as a benefit to the family ([Fortune](https://dc.fortune.com/2026/01/28/billionaire-gift-us-olympics-athletes-100-million-total-200000-apiece), [Money](https://money.com/olympic-gold-medals-worth-value/?xid=moneyrss)).
- **Talent:** More than **250 surfers aged 10 to 18** competed at the 2026 national junior championships, held at Lower Trestles itself ([USA Surfing](https://www.usasurfing.org/usa-surfing-news/usa-surfing-2026-junior-championships-crown-americas-top-young-surfers-at-lower-trestles)). As hosts, the USA is guaranteed at least one surfer per gender.

### Japan: two places already

- **Results:** Japan has the only two individual places decided so far, through Kanoa Igarashi and Mirai Ikeda at the home Asian Games.
- **Organisation and money:** Japan's elite sport is funded through the state-run Japan Sport Council. The national budget for its "competitiveness programme" for all Olympic and Paralympic sports was about **¥10.4 billion** in 2025 ([Sports Agency](https://www.mext.go.jp/sports/content/20250528-spt_kyosport-300000863_1.pdf)). We have not found the figure for surfing: the Japan Sport Council publishes the amount for each federation every year ([JSC press releases](https://www.jpnsport.go.jp/sinko/tabid/1844/Default.aspx)), but we could not open the latest list.
- **Talent:** The national federation (NSA), sponsored by the energy company ENEOS, runs [free first-time surfing sessions](https://www.tv-tokyo.co.jp/plus/external-pr/entry/45257.html) for children in three prefectures, with Olympians as instructors.
- **Medal bonuses:** The Japanese Olympic Committee pays **¥5 million for gold, ¥2 million for silver and ¥1 million for bronze**, tax-free ([Financial Field](https://financial-field.com/living/entry-228299)).

### France: coaches on every tour stop

- **Organisation:** The French Surfing Federation now has **at least one national coach at every stop of the Championship Tour**, and four national coaches, led by former pro Jérémy Florès, ran a pre-Olympic camp at Trestles in September 2026 with Kauli Vaast, Marco Mignot, Vahine Fierro and Tya Zebrowski ([AFP via Sud Radio](https://www.sudradio.fr/sport-rugby/sport/en-californie-lequipe-de-france-de-surf-prend-la-vague-des-jo-2028)).
- **Money:** High-performance sport is funded by the national sports agency (ANS), which the coaches thanked for financing the camp ([Le Petit Journal](https://lepetitjournal.com/los-angeles/actualite/la-2028-lequipe-de-france-de-surf-prend-ses-marques-trestles-452960)). We have not found a figure for surfing.
- **Talent:** France and Tahiti's federations are building a joint programme for young surfers ([Outremers360](https://outremers360.com/bassin-atlantique-appli/surf-le-pole-de-performance-franco-polynesien-lautre-heritage-des-jo)). **Kauli Vaast**, the Paris 2024 champion, says he wants to defend his title.

### Great Britain: a small budget

- **Money:** UK Sport gives surfing **£1,375,000** for 2025–2029, almost the same as for Paris (£1,404,457) (*official figure*, [UK Sport](https://www.uksport.gov.uk/sports/olympic/surfing)). Surfing is a "development sport", not a full World Class Programme. UK Sport later announced a further [uplift for several sports including surfing](https://www.uksport.gov.uk/news/2026/05/07/uk-sport-confirmed-multi-million-pound-investment-boost-to-support-athletes-preparing-for-la-2028), without giving the amount.
- **Organisation:** The governing body is Surfing England, based in Devon. No lottery-funded British surfer has yet competed at the Olympics, according to UK Sport.

### Peru, Portugal, Spain and China

- **Peru** has already won a men's team place and hosts the 2027 Pan American Games in Lima, which give one more place per gender.
- **Portugal**'s government [raised its Olympic preparation budget by 30%](https://www.portugalresident.com/portugal-approves-e30-million-for-olympic-preparations/) to €30 million for the LA28 cycle (all sports). The surfing share has not been published.
- **Spain** won team bronze at the [2025 World Surfing Games](https://stabmag.com/elsewhere/stab-high-japan-debutant-joins-curren-medina-smith-on-prestigious-isa-double-club-list/), ahead of France.
- **China** founded its national surfing team in **2018**, and Yang Siqi, from landlocked Sichuan, became China's first Olympic surfer in Paris at 15 ([Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1015521)). At 17 she won Asian Games silver.

## For every country: the IOC's new athlete grants

In June 2026 the IOC created a fund of more than **$100 million** for **$10,000 grants** that athletes can apply for after competing, officially "not prize money". Around 11,000 athletes at LA28 can apply, if they meet conditions such as never having tested positive for doping ([AP via TSN](https://www.tsn.ca/olympics/article/olympic-athletes-to-get-cash-grants-from-new-100m-fund-created-by-ioc/)).


## For the federations: Olympic TV money from 2028

Surfing, skateboarding and sport climbing were added to the Olympics through a fast-track process, and their international federations got **none of the IOC's broadcast revenue** after Tokyo or Paris. The International Surfing Association received only **US$2.7 million** from the IOC to carry it from Tokyo to Paris. Under rules from the federations' association ASOIF, a sport must have been on the programme for three Games in a row to get a full share, so the new sports get it **from LA 2028** ([Front Office Sports](https://frontofficesports.com/article/surfing-thrills-in-2024-olympics-but-faces-funding-hurdles-until-2028/)). After Tokyo, the established sports received between US$13 million and US$39 million each. How much will reach the national federations and their skaters and surfers is not yet known.
## Talent: getting young surfers through qualification

A few patterns stand out:

1. **Start young, compete internationally early.** Australia's Zoric won the world title at 14. France's Tya Zebrowski is in the Olympic training group as a teenager. Korea's Kanoa Hee-jae Palmiano switched to [homeschooling](https://www.koreajoongangdaily.com/sports/for-surfer-kanoa-heejae-palmiano-asian-games-setback-is-just-one-stop-on-road-to-olympics/12900497) to train more.
2. **The World Surfing Games matter more than before.** Seven places per gender in 2028, plus the team places, mean countries need strong national teams, not only one or two CT stars. That favours federations with deep junior programmes.
3. **Know the wave.** Lower Trestles is a fast, high-performance wave that rewards quick turns and control ([Number](https://number.bunshun.jp/articles/-/865126)). France and Brazil have already been there to train, and the USA runs its junior championships there.

## Who has the edge?

*This section is our own assessment, based on the facts above.*

- **Australia** has the most money, the biggest programme and the team places, and is the clear favourite to fill its maximum quota.
- **Brazil** has the best CT surfers, but the cap of two per gender is a bigger problem for Brazil than for anyone else.
- **Japan** is ahead on places, thanks to the home Asian Games.
- **The USA** has the home wave and host places, but its federation is only just back on its feet.
- **Smaller nations** benefit most from the revised system, through continental and universality places.
